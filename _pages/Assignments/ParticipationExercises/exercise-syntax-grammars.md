You can follow a grammar on a page without being able to write one.  Writing even a tiny grammar forces the decisions the reading makes look obvious, like what counts as a terminal, where recursion goes, and what the notation is actually buying you.  Fifteen minutes and a pencil will do it.

## The Exercises

- Write a BNF grammar for a small language of your choice: for example, signed decimal numbers, or a boolean expression with `and`/`or`/`not`.  Then extend it to EBNF and note what the EBNF made shorter.
- Given a short grammar from the reading, produce a leftmost derivation of one string it generates, and a string it *cannot* generate; explain how you know.

## What to Bring

Bring the grammar you drafted, including the rule you are least sure about.  Rough edges are fine.  That uncertain rule usually turns into the best discussion of the day.
